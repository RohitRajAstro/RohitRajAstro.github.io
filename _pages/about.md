I study how stars lose mass — and how well we can trust the measurements that tell us they do.

Right now that means Betelgeuse. At the Center for Astrophysics \| Harvard & Smithsonian, working with [Dr. Andrea Dupree](https://www.cfa.harvard.edu/people/andrea-dupree) and Dr. Paul Cristofari, I use non-LTE radiative transfer to model the surface mass ejection behind the 2019–2020 "Great Dimming" — reproducing the strange Mg II line shapes in HST/STIS spectra to pin down how much mass the star threw off, and how fast. Alongside it, I'm building a neural spectral emulator that forward-models how magnetic starspots imprint themselves on cool-star spectra, groundwork for instruments like G-CLEF on the GMT.

This September, the question scales up: I'm joining **NASA Goddard Space Flight Center** as a CRESST Research Assistant with Dr. Edmund Hodges-Kluck, studying **galactic winds** — the outflows through which whole galaxies breathe out. Stellar mass loss and galactic feedback are two ends of one story, and I want my PhD to connect them, with spectroscopy as the common thread.

The thread started at UC Santa Cruz, where two summers with Prof. Raja GuhaThakurta grew into my first first-author paper — Wolf-Rayet stars in M33, now under review at *ApJ* — and an awarded Keck/OSIRIS follow-up program. It's also where I learned my favorite question: **how much of what we measure is the star, and how much is the blend, the fiber, or the model?** I've built crowding pipelines and cross-survey comparisons to answer it, because knowing what the instrument actually sees is as fundamental as the astrophysics itself.

I came to physics the long way — from Begusarai, Bihar, where I taught myself English and then physics through online courses, eventually earning a full-tuition scholarship to [Juniata College](https://www.juniata.edu/) (with a year at the University of Leeds). The road left me cheerfully stubborn and convinced that curiosity is universal even where opportunity isn't — which is why I still run a free learning center for ~50 students back home, and volunteer with astronomy outreach wherever I live.

Off the terminal: books, bikes, and trails, plus a standing fascination with geopolitics and how ideas originate and travel — I [write about it now and then](https://medium.com/@rohit.620159).

I'm applying to astrophysics PhD programs for **Fall 2027**. If our interests overlap — stellar atmospheres and mass loss, galactic winds and feedback, spectroscopy across wavelengths, or the ML tools that connect models to data — I'd love to hear from you: [rohit.raj@cfa.harvard.edu](mailto:rohit.raj@cfa.harvard.edu).
